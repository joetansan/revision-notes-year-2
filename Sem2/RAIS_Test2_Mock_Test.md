### Question 16 — IT governance and poor logistics management (6 marks)

*A company has poor logistics management. Give THREE (3) reasons why IT governance will help this company. (6 marks)*

<details>
<summary><b>Model answer and marking guide (hidden)</b></summary>

**6 marks = 3 reasons × 2 marks. Each reason needs the module concept named (1) plus an application to
logistics (1). Do not give more than three — only three are marked.**

**Reason 1 — Strategic alignment [2]**
IT governance ensures IT is **directed by and aligned to business strategy**. In a logistics business the
systems that matter are warehouse management, transport management and route optimisation. Without
governance those are bought reactively by individual depots, so spend never attacks the real failure (late
deliveries, cost per drop). Governance links business objectives to IT goals through the goals cascade, so
investment is prioritised on the constraints that actually matter.
*(1 for "strategic alignment" / alignment of IT with business objectives; 1 for applying it to the
logistics problem.)*

**Reason 2 — Benefits realization [2]**
Governance ensures IT-enabled investments **achieve the promised benefits and deliver business value** —
not merely that the system was delivered on time and within budget. It manages the investment through the
**full economic life cycle**, especially the **adoption phase**, so the new dispatch system is actually
used by warehouse and delivery staff instead of sitting idle while paper processes continue. It also
ensures the return is **sufficient to merit the resources expended**.

**Reason 3 — Risk optimization [2]**
Governance identifies and mitigates IT risk **before it becomes a crisis**. For a logistics operation that
means loss of shipment records, a warehouse system outage halting dispatch, failure to meet customs or
regulatory requirements, and **outsourcing/3PL service levels not being met** — each of which stops
deliveries. Controls and continuity planning **limit the impact** when something does fail.

**Alternative reasons that also score (use instead of any of the above) —**
**Resource optimization**: removes duplicated or overlapping logistics systems across depots, curbs
**hidden IT spending** and prevents wastage, so the same budget buys more capability.
**Accountability / RACI**: assigns clear ownership of logistics IT decisions and outcomes, so a failed
cutover has a named owner instead of being blamed on "everybody".

*Note: IT governance does **not** promise that problems never occur. It promises a **better position to
address and react to problems, with reduced risk and limited impact** — worth a line if the question
asks you to justify the claim.*

</details>

### Question 17 — RACI in COBIT and poorly defined roles (4 marks)

*With reference to COBIT, explain what a RACI chart is. Then explain how poorly defined roles affect IT governance. (4 marks)*

<details>
<summary><b>Model answer and marking guide (hidden)</b></summary>

**Part A — What a RACI chart is in COBIT [2]**

**RACI = Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed** [1].
In COBIT, a RACI chart shows **the assignment of the various governance responsibilities to the various
roles in the organisation**, linking a **process** (or practice/activity) to each role [1]. Effective
governance depends on **comprehensive allocation of responsibilities to provide end-to-end coverage of
all governance activities** — every activity needs a clear Accountable owner and at least one
Responsible party, with consultation and information flows defined.

**Part B — How poorly defined roles affect IT governance [2]**

- **Gaps and overlaps.** Activities fall between roles or get done twice. This is the point of the
  *Responsibility Poem*: "Everybody was sure that Somebody would do it. Anybody could have done it, but
  Nobody did it." With no explicit allocation, **nobody is answerable** when the control fails. [1]
- **Accountability and decision rights collapse.** No single accountable owner means decisions stall or
  get escalated endlessly, IT decisions are taken without authority or duplicated, and blame is shifted
  rather than resolved. **Transparency of who decided what is lost.** [1]
- **Consequent effect on IT governance overall:** controls become **untestable** — auditors cannot
  trace who approved what — leading to audit findings and compliance failures. Governance exists on
  paper but is not operable, and **risk cannot be owned**, so it cannot be managed.

*Marking note: award Part B marks for any two distinct, well-explained consequences. "Nobody is
accountable" alone is worth 1; it needs the effect on governance (audit, risk ownership, decision
rights, transparency) to earn the second.*

</details>

# Appendix — the two things that cost marks

## 1. Balanced Scorecard (Q15 — this is the one that caught him)

Your slides give **one line only**:

> "The Balanced Scorecard allows for the evaluation of IT from the **user's perspective**."

It sits under **Process and service measurement techniques**, alongside the **maturity model based on ISO 15504**. If the exam asks what the Balanced Scorecard is *for* in benefits realization, that sentence is the answer.

Likely framing of the question, and what to say:

| If they ask... | Answer |
|---|---|
| What does it let you evaluate? | IT **from the user's perspective** |
| Which category of technique is it? | **Process and service measurement** |
| How is it different from the maturity model? | Maturity model = **process capability**, 6 levels (ISO 15504). Balanced Scorecard = **evaluation from the user's perspective** |
| How is it different from payback period / ROI? | Those are **financially-oriented cost-benefit** techniques; the Balanced Scorecard is a **measurement** technique |

**Context beyond the slide** (safe to know, use only if the question is broader): Kaplan & Norton's Balanced Scorecard uses four perspectives — **Financial, Customer, Internal business process, and Learning & growth** — so an organisation is measured on more than financials alone. If the question asks for four perspectives, that is the list.

## 2. RACI (Q17 — near-certain to reappear)

| Letter | Meaning | Test to remember it |
|---|---|---|
| **R** | **Responsible** | Does the work |
| **A** | **Accountable** | Answers for it — **one per activity** |
| **C** | **Consulted** | Gives input **before** the decision (two-way) |
| **I** | **Informed** | Told **after** the decision (one-way) |

In COBIT the RACI chart links a **process to roles**, and effective governance needs **comprehensive allocation for end-to-end coverage** — that phrase is worth memorising verbatim.

If asked what goes wrong without it, use the **Responsibility Poem** (Charles Osgood): Everybody was sure Somebody would do it, Anybody could have done it, but **Nobody did it** — and Everybody blamed Somebody when Nobody did what Anybody could have. Then name the consequences: **gaps and overlaps, no accountable owner, unclear decision rights, blame-shifting, untestable controls, audit findings, risk that cannot be owned.**
