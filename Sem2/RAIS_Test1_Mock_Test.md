**Q1. [Topic 1]** Which of the following best defines IT governance?

A. A set of cybersecurity controls used to protect company data
B. The system by which the use of IT within an organisation is directed and controlled
C. The process of procuring and installing IT hardware and software
D. The department responsible for resolving technical incidents in an organisation

<details>
<summary><b>Answer (hidden - click to reveal)</b></summary>

**Correct: B. The system by which the use of IT within an organisation is directed and controlled**  
This is the slide definition. IT governance is about **direction and control** of IT so that it aligns with business strategy, manages risk and delivers value — not procurement, IT support, or security tooling.
</details>

**Q8. [Topic 2]** Which of the following is a TRIGGER EVENT (rather than a pain point) for a GEIT initiative?

A. Outsourcing service levels not being met
B. Insufficient IT resources and staff with inadequate skills
C. New regulatory or compliance requirements
D. Hidden IT spending

<details>
<summary><b>Answer (hidden - click to reveal)</b></summary>

**Correct: C. New regulatory or compliance requirements**  
A **trigger event** is an internal or external *event* — merger/acquisition, new CIO/CFO/CEO, external audit, new business strategy, or **new regulatory/compliance requirements**. Hidden spending, insufficient resources and missed outsourcing SLAs are all **pain points** (conditions you are already suffering).
</details>

**Q9. [Topic 3]** In COBIT's Goals Cascade, what initiates the cascade?

A. Stakeholder drivers that influence stakeholder needs
B. IT department budget requests
C. Regulatory compliance requirements
D. Enterprise goals defined by the board

<details>
<summary><b>Answer (hidden - click to reveal)</b></summary>

**Correct: A. Stakeholder drivers that influence stakeholder needs**  
**Step 1 of the cascade is: stakeholder drivers influence stakeholder needs** — drivers such as strategy changes, changing business and regulatory environments, and new technologies. Enterprise goals are **step 2**, so 'enterprise goals defined by the board' is the off-by-one trap.
</details>

**Q10. [Topic 3]** Which barrier to strategic alignment describes the inability of senior management to communicate business strategy to operational management?

A. The conformance barrier
B. The implementation barrier
C. The specification barrier
D. The expression barrier

<details>
<summary><b>Answer (hidden - click to reveal)</b></summary>

**Correct: D. The expression barrier**  
**Expression barrier** = senior management cannot *communicate* the strategy to operational management. **Specification barrier** = IT was not involved in strategy development, so IT and business end up with two different strategies. **Implementation barrier** = the strategy cannot be implemented because of the current IT portfolio (e.g. legacy systems).
</details>

**Q11. [Topic 3]** Which of the following best describes Agility Loop 1?

A. Improving existing IT processes through automation
B. Monitoring the environment and making responsive decisions
C. Standardising the IT portfolio across all business units
D. Creating entirely new business processes and retiring obsolete ones

<details>
<summary><b>Answer (hidden - click to reveal)</b></summary>

**Correct: B. Monitoring the environment and making responsive decisions**  
**Loop 1 = monitoring and deciding**: environmental monitoring plus responsive decision making. The slides note that the **most profitable opportunities** for alignment often arise from **agile responses to new or unexpected events**. Improving existing processes is Loop 2; creating new processes is Loop 3.
</details>

**Q12. [Topic 4]** What is the key distinction between business benefits and intermediate benefits?

A. Business benefits are short-term; intermediate benefits are long-term
B. Business benefits apply to IT; intermediate benefits apply to the business
C. Business benefits create value by increasing company profits; intermediate benefits do not directly create value although they benefit one or more stakeholder groups
D. Business benefits are always financial; intermediate benefits are always non-financial

<details>
<summary><b>Answer (hidden - click to reveal)</b></summary>

**Correct: C. Business benefits create value by increasing company profits; intermediate benefits do not directly create value although they benefit one or more stakeholder groups**  
The defining test is **value creation (profit)**, not time horizon or financial vs non-financial. An **improvement in customer service that does not contribute to increased profits** is the slide's own example of an **intermediate benefit**. Benefits realization's job is to make intermediate benefits **contribute to** business benefits.
</details>

**Q13. [Topic 4]** During which phase of the full economic life cycle does an organisation achieve the expected level of performance and move the delivered capabilities into the active service portfolio?

A. The adoption phase
B. The investment phase
C. The value creation phase
D. The value sustainment phase

<details>
<summary><b>Answer (hidden - click to reveal)</b></summary>

**Correct: C. The value creation phase**  
**Value creation phase** = achieving the expected level of performance **and moving the delivered capabilities into the active service portfolio**. Investment = developing capabilities; Adoption = implementing them; Value sustainment = assuring the assets continue to create value.
</details>

**Q14. [Topic 4]** In the context of portfolio management, a portfolio is best defined as:

A. A collection of programs, projects and/or operations managed as a group to achieve strategic objectives
B. A financial investment portfolio managed by the CFO
C. A set of IT standards and policies governing project delivery
D. A collection of IT assets owned by the IT department

<details>
<summary><b>Answer (hidden - click to reveal)</b></summary>

**Correct: A. A collection of programs, projects and/or operations managed as a group to achieve strategic objectives**  
A portfolio is a collection of programs, projects and/or operations **managed as a group to achieve strategic objectives**. Critically, the components **may not necessarily be interdependent or even related** — they are grouped for strategic management, not because they are technically linked.
</details>

**Q15. [Topic 4]** At which capability maturity level (ISO 15504) is a process continuously improved to meet relevant current and projected business goals?

A. Level 5 — Optimizing process
B. Level 3 — Established process
C. Level 2 — Managed process
D. Level 4 — Predictable process

<details>
<summary><b>Answer (hidden - click to reveal)</b></summary>

**Correct: A. Level 5 — Optimizing process**  
The six levels are **0 Incomplete, 1 Performed, 2 Managed, 3 Established, 4 Predictable, 5 Optimizing**. Level 4 (Predictable) operates **within defined limits**; Level 5 (Optimizing) is **continuously improved** to meet current and projected business goals. Each level includes all the attributes of the levels below it.
</details>

### Question 17 — Barriers to strategic alignment (3 marks)

Name the **three barriers to strategic alignment** and explain each in one sentence.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer (hidden - click to reveal)</b></summary>

**Model answer and marking guide** — 1 mark per barrier, correctly named **and** correctly explained.

1. **Expression barrier** [1] — the **inability of senior management to communicate business strategy to
   operational management**, so strategy never reaches the people who must execute it.
2. **Specification barrier** [1] — **lack of IT involvement in strategy development**, resulting in **IT and
   business having two different strategies**.
3. **Implementation barrier** [1] — **difficulty implementing the strategy because of the current IT
   portfolio**, for example **legacy systems** that cannot support the intended direction.

*Common deduction:* naming "technical / financial / human" or "planning / execution / review" — these are
plausible but wrong. Only expression, specification and implementation count.
</details>

### Question 18 — Benefits realization (3 marks)

**(a) [2 marks]** Name the **five phases of the full economic life cycle** of an investment program, **in order**.

**(b) [1 mark]** A government agency invests SGD 50 million in a new digital services platform. The platform is
delivered on time and within budget, and all technical tests pass. Two years later, citizens are still using
paper-based processes and the platform sits largely unused. **Which phase failed, and why?**

<details>
<summary><b>Answer (hidden - click to reveal)</b></summary>

**Model answer and marking guide**

**(a) [2 marks]** — award **2** for all five in the correct order, **1** for three or four correct in order.

1. **Investment** — developing the necessary capabilities
2. **Adoption** — implementing the capabilities
3. **Value creation** — achieving the expected level of performance and moving the delivered capabilities
   into the active service portfolio
4. **Value sustainment** — assuring the assets continue to create value (may require additional investment)
5. **Retirement** — decommissioning the resulting assets

**(b) [1 mark]** — the **adoption phase** failed. The capabilities were **delivered but not implemented into
active use**, so **value creation never occurred** — "delivered" is not the same as "realised".

*Common deduction:* answering "investment phase" because money was spent, or "retirement phase" because the
old paper systems were not decommissioned. The investment phase succeeded (on time, on budget); retirement
cannot be the failure when adoption never happened.
</details>

## Marking Guide

**Section A (15 marks):** 1 mark per MCQ, no negative marking. 
**Section B (10 marks):** 
- **Q16 (4):** (a) 1 mark for the definition + 1 mark for all four pillars named correctly; (b) 1 mark conformance (structure/roles/remuneration) + 1 mark performance (strategy/value creation/risk appetite). 
- **Q17 (3):** 1 mark per barrier — the mark requires both the correct name **and** a correct explanation. 
- **Q18 (3):** (a) 2 marks for all five phases in the correct order (1 mark for three or four correct); (b) 1 mark for correctly identifying **adoption** and noting that delivery without active use means value creation never occurred. 

**Common deductions across the paper:**

- Naming **Software Development** as a pillar, or forgetting **Resource Optimization**.
- Attributing the Code of Corporate Governance to **SGX / IMDA / MDDI** instead of **MAS**.
- Confusing the **three pillars of sustainability (E/S/Economic)** with **ESG (E/S/Governance)**.
- Treating a **condition** (hidden IT spending, insufficient resources) as a **trigger event**.
- Answering **conformance/performance** as compliance/reporting.
- Mixing **Agility Loops 1, 2 and 3**, or the three **alignment barriers**.
- Defining benefits by **financial vs non-financial** instead of by **value creation / profit**.
- Giving an **absolute** answer where the module is deliberately qualified (e.g. 'IT governance guarantees zero incidents', 'absence of frameworks means everyone fails').
